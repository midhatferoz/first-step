Pashmina

Famous Kashmiri wool, known for its softness and luxurious texture. pashmina is a luxurious, ultra-soft shawl handwoven from the fine wool of Himalayan goats, native to Kashmir. Known for its warmth, lightweight feel, and intricate craftsmanship, each pashmina is meticulously embroidered, often taking months to complete. Its beauty and elegance make it a cherished symbol of Kashmiri artistry and heritage.

Pashmina shawls are made from the finest wool of the Capra Hircus goat, which is native to the Himalayan regions of India. The Capra Hircus goat is a hardy breed adapted to living in the extreme cold and high altitudes of the Himalayas. The goats produce a fine, downy undercoat of hair harvested by hand in the springtime when they shed their winter coat. Pashmina shawls have been a fashion statement for centuries, worn by royalty, celebrities, and fashion enthusiasts. In eastern culture, Pashmina shawls are popular as wedding accessories and are often worn by brides, grooms, and bridesmaids

Here are a few popular types: 1. Solid Pashmina shawls – the easiest to fit any style, men or women, in any culture.
2. Jamawar Pashmina shawls – ornate and intricately woven, mainly with a floral design.
3. Kani Pashmina shawls – handwoven on a wooden loom using the Kani technique.
4. Printed Pashmina shawls – printed with different designs and patterns.
5. Hand-Embroidered Pashmina shawls – with intricate and gorgeous embroidery.
Pashmina shawls are not just an accessory but an art form that has been passed down through generations. The beauty and elegance of Pashmina shawls are unparalleled, making them a timeless addition to any wardrobe
